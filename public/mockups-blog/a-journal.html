Maiden Trip: Bosnia & Slovenia

Our first overland trip ever: a last-minute pivot from Norway to the Balkans, wild horses, a scary narrow trail, and a dog who could not cross into Montenegro.

  • Summer
  • Family trip with dog
  • Mixed roads and light off-road
  • Almost 2 weeks in Bosnia
EEdwin · Voorburg · [date] · 8 min read

Just a few days before departure we were still undecided where to go. Norway had been the original plan for our maiden trip, but the rugged camping and unpredictable weather gave us pause: none of us had real experience camping in the wild, and the basic questions (bathroom, showering, safety) were still unanswered. Then, without anyone quite remembering whose idea it was, Albania came up. One look at the map and we decided to just go for it.

"This, we realised, is what overlanding actually is: no fixed plan, just going with the flow."
Part 1

A hill above the vineyards

We found a spot on a hill overlooking the Slovenian vineyards, and it was so good we stayed two nights: wine, sunset over the vines, and me discovering the joys of a long outdoor shower.

Part 2

Una park, and a place called Status Quo

Finally in Bosnia, we drove to a camping right inside the park with minimal facilities but the clearest water we had stayed by yet. Over dinner we found out we could go rafting, and booked it on the spot. Two brothers picked us up for a multi-hour tour with stops to jump in the water, fun for the whole family, Izzy included.

Outside the park we found Status Quo. To this day we have not had better facilities. The spot was never busy, which made it a real sanctuary.

Part 3

Looking for wild horses

No guided tour, just us and the car. Finding the entrance took some trial and error, and once on the plain we drove deeper and deeper without seeing anything. Then we spotted a herd, drove off-trail onto rocky terrain to get close, and got pictures and drone footage. So cool.

Part 4

The trail that got too narrow

Following a trail we had found on maps.me, things went fine until it narrowed more than we liked: shrubs, bushes, fresh scratches in the paint. The only option was reversing out, which left the clutch slipping and the car smelling scorched. Not something to repeat.

From there we headed to the highest point in Bosnia, a herders' town called [town name], reached via 40 km of bumpy gravel. Something that sounded exactly like howling wolves surrounded us that night. To this day we do not know if it was wolves or herding dogs.

Part 5

Montenegro on foot, Austria on a whim

A 4.5-hour hike took us across the border to a lake in Montenegro, dog and all, where the restaurant host checked our passports. Then a call from friends in Austria changed our plans and we drove north for a hike around the Weissee. It is good to end a trip on a high, and that hike was exactly that.

Tips

  • Travelling with a dog: sort the Montenegro paperwork (tick treatment, tether) before you leave.
  • Croatia: do not count on a restaurant seating you with a dog. Keep a supermarket picnic as plan B.
  • Outside the EU, an eSIM worked well for data.
  • Scout unmarked trails before committing a loaded truck. Ours cost paint and a squashed solar drawer.
  • Earplugs, for the nights near the herders' village.
