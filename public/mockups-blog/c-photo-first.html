Maiden Trip: Bosnia & Slovenia

By Edwin · [date] · 8 min read · Summer · family trip with dog

In short

Our first overland trip ever. We planned Norway, pivoted south days before leaving and spent almost two weeks in Bosnia, in and around Una park. Highlights: two nights above Slovenian vineyards, rafting, a herd of wild horses and a 4.5 hour hike into Montenegro. Mixed roads with some real gravel, summer, [km] in total.

Just a few days before departure we were still undecided where to go. Norway had been the original plan, but the rugged camping and unpredictable weather gave us pause: none of us had real experience camping in the wild. Then Albania came up, one look at the map, and we just went for it.

No fixed plan

We drove until around four each afternoon and found a spot for the night. A weather check showed 13 °C days and 8 °C nights at Triglav, so we turned south on the spot. A hill above the Slovenian vineyards, wine, sunset and a long outdoor shower. This, we realised, is what overlanding actually is.

Bosnia, and the dog who could not cross

Our dog Izzy could not enter Montenegro without paperwork we did not have, so we spent more time in Bosnia instead of driving through it. That turned out to be the best decision of the trip: the clearest water, a rafting day booked over dinner, and a camping called Status Quo with a free canoe the kids never wanted to leave.

Wild horses, a narrow trail and howling in the night

We found a herd on a remote plain and drove off-trail to get close. A trail from maps.me later narrowed until we had to reverse out, with a slipping clutch to show for it. Then 40 km of gravel to the highest village in Bosnia, where the only restaurant was also the only place to sleep, and something howled all night. Wolves or herding dogs: we still do not know.

Ending on a high

A 4.5 hour hike took us over the border to a lake in Montenegro, dog and all. A call from friends then pulled us north to Austria for a hike around the Weissee. It is good to end a trip on a high.

Good to know before you go

Can you take a dog to Montenegro?

Only with the right paperwork. We were missing the tick treatment and tether requirements, which reshaped our route. Check the rules well before departure.

Are the roads doable without a 4x4?

Mostly yes, but one 40 km stretch to the highest village was sharp gravel where we needed patience, and some trails were genuinely 4x4 territory.

Is wild camping allowed?

We camped on official sites and found them excellent. Rules differ per country, so check them for each border you cross.

What would you do differently?

Scout unmarked trails before committing, sort pet paperwork up front, and pack earplugs.
